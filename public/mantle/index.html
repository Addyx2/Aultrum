Mantle · The golden foundation

Everyone has a duty of care.

Mantle is the foundation beneath everything Aultrum builds. Care is a duty we all carry. AI does not carry it for us. It is a means to deliver care more accurately, and with more insight.

Principles

The duty is ours. AI helps us meet it.

Six rules every Aultrum product is held to, from the first line of code to the last phone call.

  1. 01

    The duty stays with people.

    Carers, nurses, managers, families, commissioners, and the people who build their tools all carry a duty of care. No system carries it for them, and none of ours tries to.

  2. 02

    AI makes care more accurate.

    It shows what is true about a person now, not what was written down years ago, so decisions rest on current evidence rather than old records.

  3. 03

    AI makes care more insightful.

    It sees patterns across thousands of shifts, calls and visits that no one person could hold in mind, and brings them to the person who needs them.

  4. 04

    Signals, never decisions.

    Our models produce evidence and flags. People weigh them and decide. Every action is approved by a person and recorded.

  5. 05

    Time changes people.

    Skills fade without practice, trust grows with continuity, and tiredness builds up over a run of shifts. Care that ignores time treats people as they were, not as they are.

  6. 06

    Records must not hold people back.

    When a constraint has outlived its reason, the duty of care is to notice, and to give people the evidence to release it.

The founding idea

Time has its effect on people.

This is not a metaphor. Time is the least examined variable in every system that governs people’s lives: care, work, money, health and justice. People change, situations move on, obligations pile up. A system that ignores this ends up holding people to conditions that no longer exist, or crediting them for conditions that never did.

The common thread: every system treats time as a date stamp, not as something that changes the person. Mantle treats time as the variable itself.

Deterrent obligations

When a record outlives the reason for it.

A deterrent obligation is any requirement or condition placed on a person that lasts beyond its relevance, or was never relevant, and stands in the way of their progress. They are judgements frozen in time, applied to people who are not.

The expired certificate

A CV says “five years’ experience”, but the training behind it lapsed two years ago. The five years reassures; the lapse goes unseen.

The overlooked carer

A carer isn’t matched to a client because their years served don’t fit a profile, though their recent work shows they are the better fit.

The flag that stayed

A compliance flag that no longer applies keeps someone out of work. The person changed; the record didn’t.

Part of the duty of care is noticing when an obligation has become a deterrent, and producing the evidence that lets someone release it, replace it, or look at it again.

The research

A model whose main variable is time.

Mantle’s research is building a temporal model: one that learns how a particular person changes over time, how their situation moves on, and how the obligations on them age. It is not a language model, and it is not a prediction engine. We don’t claim to have solved time; we have found a gap no existing system fills, and we are testing whether this fills it.

In

A person’s record over time: what they did, how it was assessed, how it turned out. The field it comes from, and the obligations on them, with when each began.

Model

How each attribute fades or grows for this person, how long each obligation has been relevant, and whether they are improving, steady, declining or diverging.

Out

Whether an obligation is still relevant, stale or a deterrent; the person as they are now; a suggestion to keep, release or review; and how confident it is.

The model does not make decisions. It produces signals. The people and organisations who use it decide what to do with them.

  • —Not a general-purpose AI or language model
  • —Not a “digital twin”: we don’t simulate people, we understand how time changes them
  • —Not a forecast of behaviour: it reads the time in the evidence that already exists
  • —Not a compliance tool, though compliance teams can use it

Why now

Three reasons this matters now.

Regulation is looking at time.

The CQC is moving toward continuous monitoring, and the EU AI Act asks for risk to be reassessed over time. Systems that model time are built to comply.

AI makes more data, not better judgement.

Models can write about time but rarely model it. Seeing people as they are now needs a different kind of model.

The workforce crisis is about time.

Skills fade faster than training cycles, and tiredness builds faster than rest. Retention is a question of balance over time.
