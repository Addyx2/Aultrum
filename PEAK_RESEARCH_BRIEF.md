# Brief: Recognition-Driven Retention in Social Care — How Incentive Architecture Produces Quality, Not Just Attendance

> Written for the Aultrum / Peak research programme. Produces the paper published at `/research/papers/recognition.html`.

---

## Thesis

*Recognition-based incentives — structured as a measurable, layered rewards system — do not merely hold staff. They produce the behavioural conditions under which quality care becomes sustainable: continuity, proactivity, skill growth, and the carer's sense that their work has purpose.*

The conventional frame ("pay more, retain more") is incomplete. The evidence shows that when recognition is tied to *quality signals* (not just hours), the mechanism works in both directions: incentives lift quality, and quality outcomes (in turn) produce the intrinsic motivation carers identify as their reason for staying. The result is a self-reinforcing loop — not a cost centre.

## Evidence map

The research draws on the following bodies of evidence. Each section of the paper should be grounded in at least one study.

### 1. The retention crisis is structural, not cyclical

- **NCFE Workforce Report 2026:** 53% of care workers considered leaving in the past 12 months; 1 in 4 unsure they'd recommend the career.
- **GOV.UK Adult Social Care Workforce Survey (Aug–Sep 2024):** 58.5% of domiciliary care settings reported retention as challenging. Dominant cited reason: better pay elsewhere (35.2%). *Workforce recognition* ranked as a top retention lever (17.95% of respondents).
- **EU Framework of Action on Retention & Recruitment (June 2025, EPSU / Social Employers):** Persistent staff shortages attributed to low wages, physical/mental exhaustion, and shift-work impact. 7M healthcare openings projected by 2030.
- **Skills for Care parliamentary evidence (RTR0034):** Vacancy rate running at 6.8% (~105,000 roles); turnover only falls meaningfully once pay crosses the NLW by a significant margin — meaning *non-pay levers are essential*.

### 2. Recognition is a stronger retention signal than pay alone

- **Edge Hill University / Skills for Care (March 2026):** Providers described staff recognition, supervision, and wellbeing support as "very effective" retention measures — alongside pay benchmarking. "We're not the highest paid, but we make sure we're competitive and fair." (Provider P30, survey.)
- **Social Care Wales Impact Report 2024–25:** Public opinion of care workers improved (32% more positive); "making increasing use of innovation, research and data to inform practice" positioned alongside workforce recognition as strategic pillars.
- **NCFE 2026:** "Creating supportive workplace cultures, prioritising wellbeing support for care workers, and widening access to training can play an important role in attracting and retaining talent."

### 3. Intrinsic motivation is what holds quality; recognition sustains it

- **Karaferis et al. (2022, PMC9559882):** Systematic review (3,278 hospital nurses) found recognition by seniors was one of the most important motivating factors, directly associated with organisational achievement and engagement. "Officially recognising best performance is suggested."
- **van den Heuvel et al. (2022, PMC8876425):** Mixed-methods systematic review (healthcare professionals): autonomous (intrinsic) motivation is associated with proactive behaviour → perceived quality of care. *Incentive-based quality improvements must be complemented with aspects that stimulate autonomous motivation.*
- **Performance-based payment study (2025, PMC12198835):** Higher intrinsic motivation when quality-of-care is included in performance measurement (β=0.61, p<0.05), and when incentives target groups (β=1.04, p<0.05) — exactly the design Peak proposes.

### 4. Quality care and retention share the same mechanism

- **Wang et al. (J Nurs Res 2025):** Work engagement and job satisfaction fully mediate the relationship between perceived staffing and quality of care. Engaged staff provide better care — and engaged staff stay.
- **IHI CareFront programme (Nov 2025):** Engaging direct care workers as co-designers of solutions improved both retention and care quality across pilot units; retention improvements spread alongside care redesign.
- **Barnett Papers / Daly & Fisher:** Care workers derive *intrinsic satisfaction* from performing the work itself; organisations that undermine this (through managerial neglect or pay injustice) trigger both exit and quality collapse.

### 5. The missing link: measurable recognition *produces* quality outcomes

- **Current gap:** Most retention interventions are framed as cost-reduction (lower turnover) rather than quality-production (higher care standards). The industry lacks a framework that connects incentive architecture → measurable care quality → retention.
- **Peak's contribution:** The Aultrum Layers Qualifier Engine (Shift Readiness + Rapport as v1, expanding to Competence, Compliance, Satisfaction, Purpose) provides the first framework where incentive spend is *gated by quality signals* — making every pound of incentive spend a documented investment in care quality, not a staff perk.
