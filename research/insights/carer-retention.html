Retention starts with recognition

Carers don't leave care — they leave being unseen.

The UK's care sector loses carers to burnout and to rival agencies with a shockingly low threshold of persuasion. The reasons a carer stays are rarely financial alone.

Carers report leaving agencies where their work was invisible — where no one recorded the extra hour with a frightened client, the rapport they quietly built, the small signals that their contribution mattered.

Recognition here is not a shoutout. It is a record. When applause, continuity, and quiet competence are captured and accumulate into something portable and valuable, a carer sees their own impact — and sees that staying compounds their standing.

This is the core of Honor's chorals and tiers. Not gamification for its own sake, but the machinery that makes an individual carer's worth legible to them, to their employer, and — over time — to a credit profile that can underwrite their financial life.

A carer with a Live CV and growing Honor record does not drift to the next agency. They carry a career that follows them — which is precisely why the agencies holding them should want them kept.
