Layer Paper 04 · Rapport

The bond layer.

Continuity of relationship as a measurable care quality — and why matching on it matters.

When a family loses the carer their loved one has come to trust, the loss is rarely logged as care quality. It is logged as a complaint — or worse, silently, as the family's decision to move providers.

Rapport is the accumulated trust between a carer and the people they care for. In nearly every care system it is treated as intangible: a "nice to have" that scheduling quietly tramples in the name of covering shifts.

We treat Rapport as the fourth Aultrum Layer, and as something that can be measured and — critically — matched.

What it is made of

Rapport accumulates from repeated successful interactions: consistency of carer, positive satisfaction signals, low incident rates, and open communication. Each visit either reinforces the bond or erodes it.

The measure is not a personality test. It is a record of durability: how many times has this pair worked together, how smoothly, and how positive were the outcomes?

Why matching on it is a moat

When scheduling can prefer a carer with an established rapport bond over a stranger, care stops being a random encounter and becomes a relationship. Retained clients and retained carers are the same mechanism, and rapport is the connective tissue.

Rapport is computable from the logs care already produces. It powers a simple promise: the right carer, for the right client, because of a bond that is proven — not assumed.
